# The Incredible Navigation of Arctic Terns

Arctic terns hold the record for the longest migration of any animal on Earth.
These small seabirds travel from their Arctic breeding grounds to the Antarctic
and back again each year — a round trip of roughly 70,000 km (43,000 miles).

Over their 30-year lifespan, an Arctic tern may log over 2.4 million km in
migration alone, which is equivalent to flying to the Moon and back three times.

They navigate using the sun, stars, Earth's magnetic field, and even olfactory
cues. Remarkably, young terns make their first southward migration alone, with no
adults to guide them, relying entirely on instinct encoded in their DNA.
