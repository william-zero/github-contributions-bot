# Daily Wisdom: Unlikely World Records & Unexpected Physics

## The World's Largest Snowflake Was Enormous
In January 1887, a rancher in Fort Keogh, Montana reported a snowflake 15 inches wide and 8 inches thick — a claim so absurd that the Guinness Book of World Records still lists it, mostly because nobody has bothered to disprove it at scale. Modern physics suggests large snowflakes form when many smaller crystals collide and partially melt together, so the Fort Keogh megaflake is at least theoretically plausible.

## Bananas Are Technically Radioactive
Bananas contain potassium-40, a naturally occurring radioactive isotope. This is so unremarkable that physicists invented the "Banana Equivalent Dose" (BED) as a unit to explain radiation exposure to non-scientists. One BED ≈ 0.1 microsieverts. For context, a chest X-ray is about 400 BED. You are not in danger from bananas — but you *are* mildly radioactive after eating one, which is a fun thing to announce at breakfast.
