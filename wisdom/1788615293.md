# The Paradox of the Heap

There's a logic puzzle called the Sorites paradox: if you have a heap of sand and remove one grain, you still have a heap. Remove another — still a heap. At what point does it stop being a heap?

No single grain makes the difference, yet grains clearly do matter in aggregate. The puzzle reveals that many everyday concepts (heap, bald, tall, rich) don't have sharp edges — they're inherently vague, and classical true/false logic struggles with them.

Philosophers call this the problem of vagueness. Some propose fuzzy logic, where truth isn't binary (0 or 1) but a continuum. A collection of 10,000 grains might be "heap" to degree 1.0, while 50 grains might score only 0.1. Language, it turns out, is built on gradients, not crisp lines.
