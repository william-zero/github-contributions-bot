# Weekend Thought: The Ocean's Hidden Hum

The ocean produces a continuous low-frequency hum that scientists call "microseismic noise." It's caused by waves interacting with each other and the seafloor, and it's so pervasive that seismologists have to filter it out just to hear actual earthquakes. In other words, the sea is always singing — we just can't hear it.

Every drop of water on Earth has been recycled through rain, rivers, ice, and clouds for billions of years. The water you drink today may have once quenched the thirst of a dinosaur, or floated as a cloud above a prehistoric forest.

Rest is not the absence of work — it's the ocean between the waves.
