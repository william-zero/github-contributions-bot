# The Paradox of Tolerance

Karl Popper's paradox of tolerance (1945) argues that a society cannot be
infinitely tolerant — if it extends tolerance even to those who are intolerant,
it will eventually be destroyed by the intolerant. Therefore, tolerant societies
must retain the right to suppress intolerance if necessary.

# Braess's Paradox

Adding a new road to a traffic network can sometimes make overall traffic *worse*
for everyone. This counterintuitive result (discovered by Dietrich Braess in 1968)
shows that individual rational choices can lead to collectively irrational outcomes
— a real-world Nash equilibrium gone wrong.
