# Fun Facts: Octopus Cognition & Deep Sea Bioluminescence

## Octopus Intelligence

Octopuses have distributed nervous systems — about two-thirds of their neurons are located in their arms, not their central brain. Each arm can act semi-independently, solving problems and responding to stimuli without waiting for instructions from the central brain. It's like having eight semi-autonomous assistants who sometimes don't check in with headquarters.

They also have the ability to experience REM-like sleep, during which their skin flickers through rapid color changes — possibly replaying experiences from their day, meaning an octopus might actually dream in color, despite being colorblind when awake.

## Deep Sea Bioluminescence

Over 76% of deep-sea creatures produce their own light via bioluminescence. In the mesopelagic zone (200–1000m depth), it's so pervasive that scientists believe it may be the most common form of communication on Earth by sheer number of signaling events. Fireflies are the celebrities, but deep-sea copepods, squid, and jellyfish are doing the real heavy lifting.

The anglerfish's iconic lure glows thanks to symbiotic bacteria — the fish itself cannot produce the light. The bacteria live in a small pouch near the lure and get a warm, nutrient-rich home in exchange for being a living lantern.
