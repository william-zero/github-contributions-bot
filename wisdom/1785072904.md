# Weekend Wisdom: The Great Molasses Flood

On January 15, 1919, a massive storage tank in Boston burst open, unleashing
2.3 million gallons of molasses through the streets at an estimated 35 mph.
The wave reached heights of 8 to 15 feet and stretched about 160 feet wide.
Twenty-one people were killed and 150 were injured in what became one of the
strangest industrial disasters in history.

To this day, some residents claim that on hot summer days, the neighborhood of
Boston's North End still smells faintly of molasses.

> "Moving at the speed of molasses in January" took on a whole new meaning.
