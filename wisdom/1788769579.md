# The Ship of Theseus and Digital Identity

The Ship of Theseus is an ancient paradox: if you replace every plank of a ship one by one, is it still the same ship? The Greeks debated this; modern philosophers still do.

**The digital twist:** your files on a computer aren't stored as one continuous thing — they're fragments scattered across a drive, reconstructed each time you open them. The "file" you see is more like a performance than an object. Every time you copy a file, the copy is bit-for-bit identical to the original. So which one is the *real* file?

**Bonus weirdness:** DNA replication introduces ~1 error per billion base pairs. Over a lifetime, most of your cells have slightly different DNA than you were born with. By the Ship of Theseus standard, you're a slow-motion paradox piloting a meat vessel made of imperfect copies.
