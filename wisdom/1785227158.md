# The Turing Test & Unexpected Animal Intelligence

## Alan Turing's Original Test

When Alan Turing proposed his famous "Imitation Game" in 1950, he estimated that by the year 2000, machines would be able to fool human judges 30% of the time in a 5-minute conversation. He was surprisingly close — modern LLMs routinely pass informal versions of the test.

## Crows: The Feathered Philosophers

Crows (family Corvidae) have demonstrated:
- **Tool manufacturing**: New Caledonian crows bend wires into hooks to retrieve food — something only great apes and humans were thought capable of
- **Causal reasoning**: They understand *why* water rises when you drop objects, and choose denser materials to raise water levels faster
- **Grudge memory**: Crows remember human faces for years, warn their offspring about "dangerous" humans, and have been documented holding "funerals" for deceased flock members

## The Octopus Nervous System Anomaly

Roughly **two-thirds of an octopus's neurons** are not in its brain — they're distributed throughout its eight arms. Each arm can taste, touch, and problem-solve semi-independently, even when severed from the body. This makes octopuses a kind of living, distributed computer network.

## Bonus: The Pigeon Oncologist

In a 2015 study, pigeons trained for a few weeks achieved **85% accuracy** detecting breast cancer in mammograms — on par with trained human radiologists. They were particularly good at identifying benign vs. malignant micro-calcifications. The pigeons apparently never formed a union to demand fair wages.
